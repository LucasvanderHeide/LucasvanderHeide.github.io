4 Free-choice Assignment: LabPrepMapper

4.1 Choosing a New Data Science for Biology Skill

For my free-choice assignment, I want to learn Python programming and explore how AI can support laboratory preparation. I plan to use Python together with LangChain and Ollama. My goal is to develop a small prototype called LabPrepMapper.

As a Life Sciences student training to become a biomedical laboratory scientist, I am interested in both molecular biology and data science. During Data Science for Biology 1 and 2, I learned to program in R and Bash. I would also like to learn Python because it is widely used in data science and has many applications in Life Sciences. In addition, I am interested in AI applications and in how they can be connected to existing programming tools to perform specific tasks.

I do not aim to become a Python expert within this assignment. Instead, I want to learn the Python skills that are necessary to build and understand a small working application. I will therefore learn relevant Python concepts alongside the development of LabPrepMapper, rather than completing a large part of a Python course before starting the project.

4.2 Development of the Project Idea

4.2.1 Initial idea

My initial idea was to develop a locally run AI assistant that could help students prepare laboratory protocols. The user would provide a protocol as a PDF document, for example, exported from LabArchives. Python functions would scan the document for a selection of predefined, safety-related topics. Examples include:

  • Whether H-statements and P-statements are mentioned.
  • Whether personal protective equipment is documented.
  • Whether first-aid information and emergency procedures are included.
  • Whether waste disposal is addressed.
  • Whether relevant safety documentation, such as an SDS, is referred to.

If a topic could not be detected, LabPrepMapper would flag it as something the student may want to review. A flag would not automatically mean that information is missing or that the protocol is incorrect. The intention was to support students in organising their preparation and noticing topics that deserve attention.

4.2.2 Teacher feedback and further reflection

I discussed this idea with my teacher. The teacher suggested making the scope smaller by focusing on the chemicals used in a protocol and looking up their H- and P-statements and relevant safety information in a limited selection of reliable sources. This was a useful suggestion because it offered a concrete way to connect laboratory preparation with Python functions and an AI model.

After considering the suggestion further, I reflected on the role I wanted LabPrepMapper to have. I do not want students to treat an AI-generated output as a replacement for their own preparation or for checking the original documentation. I also realised that automatically interpreting and combining safety information from different documents could make the prototype more complex than I can reasonably validate within the available 32 hours. Differences between documents may depend on the exact product, its composition or the document version. A simple summary could give an unjustified impression that the information has been fully checked.

For this reason, I have decided to return to a refined version of my original idea. LabPrepMapper will focus on mapping and organising safety-related topics that are mentioned in a laboratory preparation document, and highlighting topics that may need the user’s attention. It will not independently determine whether a protocol is safe, complete or correct. It will also not generate chemical-specific safety instructions.

This is a deliberate scope decision based on my own reflection about what would be useful, realistic and responsible to develop within the available time. The teacher’s suggestion helped me think more specifically about the safety-related purpose of the tool, even though I have chosen a different technical implementation for the first prototype.

4.3 Aim and Intended Functionality

The aim of LabPrepMapper is to support students when preparing a laboratory protocol by creating a structured overview of selected topics in their document. It is intended as a preparation aid, not as an automated safety assessment or approval system.

The first prototype will have the following workflow:

  1. The user provides a text-based PDF or a text file containing a laboratory preparation document.
  2. Python extracts the text and keeps track of the page on which it was found.
  3. Custom Python functions search for predefined terms and patterns related to selected document topics.
  4. The program records which topics were detected and on which pages they appeared.
  5. A locally running language model, accessed through LangChain and Ollama, can turn the checklist results into a short, readable summary.
  6. LabPrepMapper saves the results as a structured Markdown report for the user to review.

The first set of topics will be limited to:

  • References to H-statements and P-statements.
  • Personal protective equipment.
  • First aid and emergency procedures.
  • Waste management.
  • Ventilation and the work environment.
  • References to SDS or other safety documentation.

The exact search terms and patterns will be defined in Python and can be adapted during testing. The program will report where it detected a term or pattern. It will not decide whether the information is adequate. For example, detecting the word “gloves” does not establish that the correct protective equipment has been specified. Likewise, not finding a particular keyword does not prove that the topic is absent; the document may use different wording.

The AI component will be used to summarise the checklist findings in clear language. In the first version, the language model will receive the structured checklist results rather than the full experimental procedure. This keeps the AI component focused on organising the findings and reduces the chance that it will interpret experimental details as safety advice.

4.4 Scope and Responsible Use

To keep the project manageable and to make the output clear to users, the first prototype will have several limitations:

  • It will search for predefined terms and patterns; it will not understand every possible way of describing a safety topic.
  • It will not assess whether a protocol is safe, scientifically correct or complete.
  • It will not determine which H-statements or P-statements apply to a particular chemical.
  • It will not generate chemical-specific handling, storage, personal protective equipment, first-aid or waste-disposal instructions.
  • It will not automatically compare or combine information from multiple SDS documents.
  • A detected mention is not proof that the topic has been adequately addressed, and a missing match is not proof that the topic is absent.
  • The AI-generated summary may contain mistakes and must be checked against the program’s actual findings.

The user remains responsible for reviewing the original protocol, consulting the applicable institutional documentation and discussing uncertainties with a qualified supervisor. LabPrepMapper is intended to make preparation more structured and to encourage critical review, not to replace it.

The prototype will initially focus on text-based PDFs. Scanned documents and optical character recognition are outside the first scope. Testing will use fictional documents or documents that are explicitly approved for this purpose. Any example shared in the public portfolio will be synthetic or anonymised and will not contain personal, confidential or restricted laboratory information.

4.5 Learning Objectives and Technical Approach

I will learn and apply the following skills:

  • Python fundamentals: variables, strings, conditions, loops, functions and working with lists and dictionaries.
  • File handling: reading text files and extracting text from text-based PDFs.
  • Text processing: searching for predefined terms and simple patterns.
  • Error handling: dealing with missing, unsupported or unreadable files.
  • Ollama: running a small language model locally.
  • LangChain: connecting the local model to Python code and exploring how Python functions can be made available as tools.
  • Testing and evaluation: checking whether the program detects expected topics and whether the generated summary accurately reflects the checklist output.

Python will form the basis of the application. I will use the pypdf package to extract text from PDF files. I will use Ollama to run a small local language model and the langchain-ollama integration to communicate with it through LangChain.

The first working version is a Python application with a local model call. It is not yet a fully autonomous agent. I will explore LangChain tool calling as part of the assignment, but I will only add it if I can implement and explain it reliably within the available time. A clear and testable application is more important than adding agentic functionality that I cannot properly validate.

I started working on Python later than I originally intended because of the workload from other course components and the parallel Projecticum. I have decided to adapt the learning process instead of first completing a large part of the Python MOOC before beginning the application.

So far, I have:

  • Studied approximately two hours of Python, including introductory material and part of the next section of the MOOC.
  • Installed Visual Studio Code, Python and the Python extension.
  • Created a project folder and a virtual environment for LabPrepMapper.
  • Installed Ollama and downloaded a small local language model (Qwen 3.5 2B).
  • Installed the Python packages for LangChain, the Ollama integration and PDF processing.
  • Created an initial Python script that reads a text file and extracts protocol steps.
  • Tested a connection between Python, LangChain and the local Ollama model.

The first test successfully read a fictional text protocol, extracted its steps and sent them to the local model for a basic overview. This showed that the main tools can communicate with each other. The next development step is to implement the predefined checklist and test PDF text extraction.

4.7 Planning and Approach

A total of 32 hours is allocated to the free-choice assignment. Since I started later than planned and have already made progress with the setup and first prototype, I have changed my approach. I will learn Python concepts alongside development and focus on the skills that are directly relevant to the next part of the application.

This is an adaptive approach: I will use the MOOC as a reference and learning resource, but I will not complete every exercise if I already understand and can apply the relevant concept. When I encounter a Python concept that I need for the application, I will study that topic and apply it directly. This should make the learning process more efficient and help me build a working product within the available time.

To complete the assignment, I will use:

  • A computer capable of running Python and a small Ollama model locally.
  • Visual Studio Code as the development environment.
  • Python and a project-specific virtual environment.
  • Ollama with a small local language model.
  • The langchain and langchain-ollama Python packages.
  • The pypdf package for extracting text from PDF documents.
  • Fictional or explicitly approved, anonymised test documents.

4.9 Development and Final Product

The final product will be a self-developed Python application called LabPrepMapper. It will read a text-based laboratory preparation document, search for selected safety-related topics, record where those topics are mentioned and generate a structured report. A local language model, accessed through LangChain and Ollama, will help summarise the checklist findings in readable language.

The prototype will demonstrate my ability to use Python for file processing, text analysis, data structures and custom functions. It will also demonstrate how a locally running language model can be connected to a Python application. If time and technical feasibility allow, I will explore a limited tool-calling step through LangChain. This is an additional learning goal rather than a requirement for the first working version.

I will test the program using fictional or approved documents with known examples of topics that should be detected. I will compare the output with the document manually to see whether the expected terms and pages are found. I will also test cases where a topic is not mentioned, where alternative wording is used, and where a file cannot be read. I will evaluate false matches, missed matches, PDF extraction problems and whether the AI summary accurately reflects the checklist results.

The final evaluation will focus on the technical performance and limitations of the prototype. It will not attempt to validate the safety of a laboratory protocol. Possible improvements for future development may include better recognition of alternative wording, a more user-friendly interface or, after further discussion and validation, a carefully limited way of pointing users towards approved reference documentation.

4.10 Reflection on the Development Process and Use of AI

During this assignment, I have adjusted the original plan based on teacher feedback, further reflection and the technical work carried out so far. Initially, I expected to spend the first part of the assignment mainly learning Python and only start developing the application later. I realised that this would not be the most efficient approach for my goal. I therefore started building a small prototype while learning the Python concepts needed for each component.

I also refined the intended role of LabPrepMapper. The initial concept and the teacher’s suggestion both focused on making safety-related information easier to find. After considering the responsibility of the user and the available development time, I chose to make the first version a document preparation aid that maps and flags topics rather than an application that interprets safety information or judges whether a protocol is safe. This keeps the tool useful while making its limitations clearer.

Generative AI tools, including ChatGPT, are used as learning and development support. They may help me understand Python concepts, troubleshoot errors, discuss the structure of the application and suggest code. I remain responsible for reviewing, testing and adapting the code. I will document the parts I have used, make sure I understand how they work and only include functionality that I can explain during the assessment.
